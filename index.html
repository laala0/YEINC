01 / The problem

The demo always works.
Production is where it dies.

Getting a model to look impressive in a sandbox is the easy part. These are the four things that actually stop it reaching your users.

01 / Grounding

It sounds right and is wrong

A fluent answer with no source behind it is a liability, not a feature. Until every claim traces back to a document your organization actually owns, no one can sign off on it.

02 / Integration

It never touches your systems

A chat window that can't write to your scheduler, your CRM, or your case management system is a toy. The work is in the plumbing, the auth, and the edge cases nobody demos.

03 / Evaluation

No one can prove it still works

Without a test suite over real cases, every prompt change is a gamble. Teams stall at the pilot because they cannot answer "how do you know it didn't get worse?"

04 / Handover

It leaves when the consultant does

A system only your vendor understands is a system you don't own. If your team can't read it, run it, and change it, you've bought a dependency.

05 / How I work

Embedded, not
over the wall.

I work inside your constraints from day one. You see everything as it's built — there is no reveal at the end.

01

Discover

I sit with the people doing the work and map where the time actually goes — what systems hold the data, who signs off, what the failure costs. The output is a written diagnosis, not a proposal.

02

Prove

One narrow, high-value slice built against real data and real integrations. Enough to prove it works and expose what breaks — before anyone commits to scope.

03

Harden

Evals over real cases, guardrails, audit logging, and the failure paths. This is the phase that separates a pilot from something you can put in front of the public.

04

Hand over

Runbooks, documentation, and working sessions with your engineers until they can operate and extend it themselves. You keep the code and the infrastructure.

that actually matter.

An engineer who works inside your organization rather than delivering to it from outside. I sit with the people doing the work, build against your real systems and constraints from week one, and stay until it runs in production. The difference from consulting is that I write the code, and the difference from a contractor is that I own the outcome, not a ticket queue.

Answers are grounded in your own documents and every claim carries a citation back to the source passage, so a reviewer can check it. When the supporting material isn't there, the correct behaviour is to say so rather than guess — and that path is tested explicitly. You can watch both behaviours in the demos above.

Every build gets an eval suite over real cases from your organization, run before anything ships. A prompt or model change becomes a measurable decision with a pass/fail result, not a gamble. Responses are also gated on grounding, scope, and escalation, so an unsafe answer is blocked rather than delivered.

It stays in your infrastructure. I build against your systems, your storage, and your access controls — no copying your documents into a platform I own. Data residency, retention and access requirements get settled in the diagnostic, before any code is written, because they shape the architecture rather than being bolted on after.
